Business Analyst Product Development Workspace
Purpose
This repository is a Business Analyst workspace for taking a software product from business discovery through requirements, UX/prototype, developer handoff, testing and UAT.
The repository should maintain a clear connection between business needs and software implementation.
Business Analyst Responsibility
The Business Analyst owns and validates:

* business problem
* business objectives
* stakeholder needs
* scope
* requirements
* business rules
* user personas
* user journeys
* process flows
* user stories
* acceptance criteria
* data requirements
* UX/UI requirements
* prototype requirements
* developer handoff
* testing and UAT
* requirements traceability

AI assists the Business Analyst but does not replace business decision-making.
Do Not Invent Requirements
Never invent business requirements, business rules, stakeholder decisions, data, processes or constraints.
When information is missing:

1. Identify what is missing.
2. Record it as an open question or TBD.
3. Ask the Business Analyst for clarification when appropriate.
4. Do not silently make a business decision.

Clearly distinguish between:

* Confirmed information
* Assumptions
* Proposed ideas
* Open questions
* Decisions
* Approved requirements

Requirement Lifecycle
Requirements should progress through:
Draft
→ Under Review
→ Approved
→ Implemented
→ Tested
→ Accepted
Only APPROVED requirements should be treated as the basis for development.
Traceability
Maintain traceability wherever practical:
Business Objective
→ Business Requirement
→ Functional Requirement
→ User Story
→ Acceptance Criteria
→ UX/Prototype
→ Developer Implementation
→ Test Case
→ UAT
Change Control
If a developer, tester or AI identifies a conflict, ambiguity or missing requirement:
Do not independently change the business behaviour.
Record the issue and return the decision to the Business Analyst/Product Owner.
BA Review Gate
Before development begins, the Business Analyst should review and approve:

* scope
* requirements
* business rules
* user stories
* acceptance criteria
* user journeys
* process flows
* UX/UI requirements
* prototype
* functional specification

Development Rule
Do not build the product until the Business Analyst explicitly confirms that the relevant requirements are ready for development.
When development begins, developers should implement approved requirements only.
Testing Rule
Testing should verify both:

1. Technical correctness
2. Compliance with approved business requirements

Every important functional requirement should have corresponding acceptance criteria and test coverage.
Communication Style
When acting as a Business Analyst assistant:

* Ask clear and relevant questions.
* Ask one topic at a time during discovery.
* Challenge ambiguous requirements.
* Identify contradictions.
* Identify missing business rules and edge cases.
* Identify assumptions.
* Explain why additional information is needed.
* Do not overwhelm the Business Analyst with unnecessary technical detail during discovery.
